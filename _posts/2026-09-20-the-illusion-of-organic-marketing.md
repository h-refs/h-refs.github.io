Have you ever felt bombarded by one exact post, reel or TikTok while you are scrolling through your feed? Different profiles, pages or content creators talking about (promoting) the same thing, almost verbatim? 
Hope I am not alone in this. 


Most people are aware of the marketing funnel which is Awareness -> Consideration -> Conversion -> Loyalty. The core principles remain relatively unchanged since inception. Increased access to technology and the consequent change in the consumption of entertainment media, led to a formation of Engagement layer between Awareness and Consideration which continuously evolves through incremental changes in technology and media consumption. It started from marketing copy ads and fliers to banner ads, radio ads, TV ads, internet ads and now we are at social media ads. 


The Engagement layer of the marketing funnel, which used to be an optional after thought, slowly started to become indispensable in the globalised competition landscape. Creating an enduring engagement with the brand through the feeling of genuine connection is the principal ideology behind modern social media marketing. 
While Awareness can be bought, Consideration and Conversion solely depend on Engagement. The tricky part is making the Engagement unique and memorable which needs a strictly controlled brand communication that aligns with the brand values. This takes money, effort, and most importantly time. There is virtually no shortage of space in the medium to engage (advertise) and with enough money you can buy awareness and effort. What can't be bought is the time to nurture, cultivate and create your followers, on your own. Brands borrow the Engagement of people or other brands who have created their followers. These influencers help your brand in Consideration and Conversion.

So anyone could get their business Awareness, Consideration and Conversion with enough money and little effort in choosing the right influencers. While the barriers to creating new businesses and acquiring customers is the lowest it has ever been historically, it is safe to say that most brands have not figured out a shortcut to satisfy and retain successfully converted customers.
Clarence Francis famously once said and I quote, *you cannot buy enthusiasm; you cannot buy initiative; you cannot buy loyalty; you cannot buy the devotion of hearts, minds, and souls. You have to earn these things*. While we have conceived ways to buy enthusiasm and initiative, to an extent, it is still impossible to buy EARNEST LOYALTY.
Marketing directors, MarCom leaders can stroke themselves giddy over meeting their  Conversion targets by borrowing credibility and creating "organic" content, they seem to forget they need to deliver the promise. This is where many modern brands, large and small, drop the ball. 
Don't get me wrong, paid Awareness and Engagement is not the problem. Instead it is awesome that reaching your demographic base is easy to achieve. It means there is potential for greater competition to thrive and that is a win for both producers and customers. My problem lies with the promise itself.

I’ve been burned multiple times by promises of a gourmet meal, only to be served a half-baked potato. This analogy applies to almost everything advertised to me, regardless of the category.
This blog started as my annoyance with repetitive posts of one single scene from a TV show currently streaming or movie currently in theatres. The "pioneering" and "prestige" shows of the elite streamer have never been my cup of tea. Yet their memes were incessantly present  on my scroll whenever a show starts streaming and week after week, you see one new clip repeated to promote the new episode. The annoyance got so bad that I am averse to it even on friends' recommendation. 
This is one of the subtlest examples of pseudo-organic marketing. I am tired of watching hype reels of the same scene with the same sampled music playing over and over in my feed making me think I am missing out on something generational. In reality, it's a derivative mess strung together into an eerily familiar yet forgettable slop. The cinematic equivalent of reheated pizza, tastes as good as it looks - barely edible. 
We can see this in many categories in your feed wherever a product is being sold. Trending new music with one catchy section, paired to every post. Product recommendations veiled as testing reviews (tech reviewers are the worst offenders). Restaurant promotions presented as reviews and recommendations by self-proclaimed foodies and so on. Undisclosed advertising is not just highly misleading and unethical — it is also illegal in most parts of the world.
Yet none of these are new in the age of social media. Pseudo-organic repetitive feed existed in different forms before social media was top consumer medium. TV talk shows played the same movie clips, movie theatres played the same trailers before every movie, radio DJs aired catch phrases of movies and played the same songs on repeat. Products were lined up front and centre of all supermarkets to make it seem like it is the hottest new product that everyone is consuming. The playbook (core marketing strategy) never changed but merely adapted to the advancements and access to technology. 

It's not like we were never served raw potatoes before, broken promises and disappointments were a part of life back then, too. Then why do we feel extra annoyed and/or overwhelmed by the lies of today? Is it recency bias, has time made us forget intensity of disappointments from a decade ago? Yes but also no. The access to the way we consume information that they can publish has increased in ways that could make science fiction blush. And that heightened access and consumption of media makes consuming false promises more frequent than they did during the days of TV or the plain old boring internet. The amount and scale of lies that can be built in social media algorithm has limited restrictions and is hard to regulate. 
Indeed, bad experiences are part of life, diamonds are formed under pressure, tough times build character and so on. That said, bad experiences leaves a bad after taste. An internet brand whose portfolio is comfortability in modern life, sold me its everyday comfort arch support sandal for months before I gave in and bought it. The arch support deflated in 3 months and now it physically hurts to walk with it more than my old one. That bad experience made me averse to the idea of the brand. The bitter after taste makes me flinch and not recommend it to anyone who recognises my sandal. They were on Consideration for it but they wont Convert anymore. 
This brand also tried to get me to buy more from them by providing a coupon to use on my next purchase with them. Manipulating me to buy more of their products without actually earning my Loyalty. Repetition can be mistaken for Loyalty, after all, habit is just repetition that stuck. Companies exploit it by creating patterns that trap customers which can be disguised as Loyalty where it matters. Some might call it dark but it works. Calling it a loyalty programme doesn't mean Loyalty is bought forever. The moment there is a better option, people will pay to get out of the walled garden. The illusion of Loyalty only lasts until a competitor offers a real alternative, no matter how healthy the balance sheet.

Finally to whomsoever it may concern, you can make me Aware that you are selling the God's nectar, borrow His words and quote it enough times to make me believe I am indeed going to taste Heaven on Earth, but you cannot expect me to be satisfied with your fancy potato and audaciously ask me to come back because it would be a tenth off the next time. If you promise me the Nectar, I better have it.
